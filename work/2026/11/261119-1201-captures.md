## Intent

The person found option A the most reasonable answer to finding 3 of the home's assessment `261118-2152`, that the
hub's writes to the home other than the registry's moves have no path, but pointed out that an idea could already be
captured several times a day. With option A, a request that starts at once writes nothing in the home (the home's item
`261119-1039`), while what is captured for later, such as an idea, still reaches the home in a work item, one pull
request each. Asked on 2026-11-19, in the chat of a session, choosing option A among those offered for finding 3.

### Addenda

On 2026-11-19, in the chat of the session that wrote the strategy, the person found its first design (one open pull

## Scope and acceptance criteria

### In scope

- How a capture reaches the home's default branch: an idea or a request kept for later in `backlog/<project>/`, and,
  in the same way, an item of `inbox/` with its verdict and a lesson of a technology or of a host.
- The workflow's sections that say it (1, 3.3, 5, 8 and 20 as far as they name the home's writes), the core rules if
  a rule on records or commits changes, the kit's checks of records and commits if they must accept the design, and a
  script of `hub/` with its tests if code makes the capture.

### Out of scope

- The registry's moves, which keep their items made by `hub/projects.mjs`.
- The home's own instructions: once this item is merged, a home item points its "Dispatch" step to the capture.
- The inbox, the reader and the radar themselves, which phase 3 builds.

### Boundaries

- The home's default branch still changes only through pull requests that the person merges.
- Every capture is on a branch of the hosting service the moment it is made: nothing captured lives only on the host.
- The kit's rules name no hosting service.

### Acceptance criteria

1. The workflow says, in one place, how a capture reaches the home, and that several captures share one pull request.
2. A capture made while such a pull request is open joins it; one made when none is open starts a new one.
3. The kit's checks pass on a branch made that way, and fail on what the design does not allow.
4. If a script makes the capture, its tests cover a first capture, a second one joining it, and one after the merge.
5. The profile's `verify` passes.

## Strategy

Designs, each with what it costs the person:

- **A. One open capture item at a time** (recommended). A capture is a commit on the branch of the home's open
  capture item; the first capture after a merge creates the item, its branch and its record, and the hub opens its
  pull request. The person merges it when they like, once a day or once a week, and sees every capture in it before it
  lands. Made by code, as the moves are: `hub/capture.mjs <file> <destination>` commits the file on that branch, in a
  temporary worktree, and the hub pushes. The record is written whole by the script, as a move's is; what changes in
  the rules is that a capture item's closing commit is not its last: captures after it add files only under the
  capture folders, and the checks of records and commits accept exactly that.
- **B. A capture item per day.** The same, with the item closed at the end of each day and a new one the next. One
  pull request per day even when the person merges less often; nothing gained over A.
- **C. Captures outside the repository until a scheduled item commits them.** No rule changes, but a capture lives
  only on the host until then, which the boundaries exclude.

With A, the work is: the workflow's section on the home's writes (a subsection of 5, "Capture", that sections 1, 3.3,
8 and 20 point to); the exception in the core rules' "Commits and branches" for a capture item; `checks/record.mjs`
and `checks/commits.mjs` accepting commits after the closing one only on a capture item and only under its folders;
`hub/capture.mjs` with its tests, sharing the worktree code of `hub/projects.mjs`.

Assumptions: the home's other items (moves, upkeep) touch no capture folder, so an open capture pull request does not
conflict with them; a capture is a new file, never an edit, so two captures never conflict with each other.

Risks: the guard and the hosting service see an open pull request that keeps growing; the person merges it with
captures they have already read one by one. A capture that turns out wrong is corrected by another capture, as the
branch only grows.

Cost: about one and a half full items, since the rules, two checks and a new script change.
